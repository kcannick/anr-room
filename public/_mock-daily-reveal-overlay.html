What happens, in order

  1. Card slides in. Record title, artist, handle. The average reads 0.0.
  2. The average counts up to the real number (about 1.4s, ticking). It lands with a chord; the meaning of its whole number prints under it ("Potential Single").
  3. The scale strip shows the purple marker where he predicted; the green marker slides to the average and the gap is shaded.
  4. The three tiles: rated · predicted · off by.
  5. Points and the round grade pop in together. A bullseye says BULLSEYE +125; a negative round turns the points block red.
  6. The footer: today's running total, grade, N of N, and one tick per record.
  7. Holds about 8s, fades out, back to transparent. Replays only on the next press.

The report view

  1. From the daily page, after the reveal, Show report puts page 1 of the record's Track Report on the overlay: the real pages the artist gets by email, rendered on demand from the final numbers.
  2. ◀ ▶ on the daily page (or two Stream Deck keys) turn the pages; the strip at the foot shows where you are. The host talks through it: the decision, the split, against the room, what to spend.
  3. Page count varies by record (4–9): a page with no data drops out, comments take what they need.
  4. Close returns to transparent. This is the sales floor: every artist who submits gets this report. The share page already carries the submit line, so the overlay adds no call to action of its own.

Rules it keeps

  1. Fires only after the public close, only on the admin's press, only for his own vote. The server refuses everyone else, so the seal stays a server rule.
  2. The number shown is the round's real, final average: nobody else can vote in the grace window.
  3. No rank on this card. "You'd sit #N today" is a separate decision.
  4. Audio is WebAudio from this page, so OBS carries it; the daily page stays silent.
